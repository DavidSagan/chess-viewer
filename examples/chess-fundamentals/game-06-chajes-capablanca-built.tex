Of all the variations of the French Defence I like this best, because it gives Black more chances to obtain the initiative.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5}{5.e5}}

Though I consider \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_exd5}{5.exd5} the best move, there is much to be said in favour of this move, but not of the variation as a whole, which White adopted in this game.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6}{5...h6} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2}{6.\figB d2} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3}{\figB xc3} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3}{7.bxc3} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4}{\figN e4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4}{8.\figQ g4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8}{\figK f8}}

The alternative, \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_g6}{8...g6}; leaves Black's King's side very weak. White by playing \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_g6_h4}{9.h4} would force Black to play \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_g6_h4_h5}{9...h5}; and later, on White's Bishop by going to d3, would threaten the weakened g-pawn. By the text move Black gives up Castling, but gains time for an attack against White's centre and Queen's side.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1}{9.\figB c1} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5}{c5}}

Threatening \figQ a5 and stopping thereby White's threat of \figB a3. It demonstrates that White's last move was a complete loss of time and merely weakened his position.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3}{10.\figB d3} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5}{\figQ a5} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2}{11.\figN e2} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4}{cxd4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O}{12.0--0} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3}{dxc3} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4}{13.\figB xe4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4}{dxe4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4}{14.\figQ xe4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6}{\figN c6}}

\ChessDiagram{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6}{\includegraphics[width=\boardwidth]{diagrams/board-c3cdae7c8418}}}

Black has come out of the opening with a Pawn to the good. His development, however, has suffered somewhat, and there are Bishops of opposite colour, so that it cannot be said as yet, that Black has a won game; but he has certainly the best of the position, because, besides being a Pawn to the good, he threatens White's e-pawn, which must of course be defended, and this in turn will give him the opportunity to post his Knight at d5 via e7. When the Black Knight is posted at d5, the Bishop will be developed to c6 via d7, as soon as the opportunity presents itself, and it will be Black that will then have the initiative, and can consequently decide the course of the game.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1}{15.\figR d1}}

To prevent \figN e7; which would be answered by \figN xc3, or still better by \figB a3. The move, however, is strategically wrong, since by bringing his pieces to the Queen's side, White loses any chance he might have of making a determined attack on the King's side before Black is thoroughly prepared for it.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6}{15...g6} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4}{16.f4} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4_Kg7}{\figK g7} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4_Kg7_Be3}{17.\figB e3}}

Better would have been \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4_Kg7_a4}{17.a4}, in order to play \figB a3. The White Bishop would be much better posted on the open diagonal than here, where it acts purely on the defensive.

\Move{\href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4_Kg7_Be3_Ne7}{17...\figN e7} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4_Kg7_Be3_Ne7_Bf2}{18.\figB f2} \href{https://lichess.org/analysis/pgn/e4_e6_d4_d5_Nc3_Nf6_Bg5_Bb4_e5_h6_Bd2_Bxc3_bxc3_Ne4_Qg4_Kf8_Bc1_c5_Bd3_Qa5_Ne2_cxd4_O-O_dxc3_Bxe4_dxe4_Qxe4_Nc6_Rd1_g6_f4_Kg7_Be3_Ne7_Bf2_Nd5}{\figN d5}}

This Knight completely paralyses the attack, as it dominates the whole situation, and there is no way to dislodge it. Behind it Black can quietly develop his pieces. The game can now be said to be won for Black strategically.
